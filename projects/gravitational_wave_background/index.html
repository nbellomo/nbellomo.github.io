The Astrophysical Gravitational-Wave Background

Mapping The Anisotropies Of The Faint Hum Of Merging Black Holes

Every second, somewhere in the Universe, two black holes or neutron stars merge. Most of these events are individually undetectable, but together they form a persistent, stochastic hum: the astrophysical gravitational-wave background. Like the cosmic microwave background, this signal is not perfectly uniform across the sky — its anisotropies trace both the properties of the sources and the large-scale structure they inhabit.

My work focuses on modeling these anisotropies from first principles, including all the projection effects — such as Kaiser and Doppler contributions — that arise because we observe the background from within a perturbed universe (Bertacca et al., 2020). Together with my collaborators I developed CLASS_GWB, a public extension of the CLASS Boltzmann code that computes the angular power spectrum of the astrophysical gravitational-wave background for arbitrary source populations and detector networks (Bellomo et al., 2022).

Current directions include forecasting the detectability of these anisotropies with next-generation interferometers and pulsar timing arrays, and using them as a novel tracer of the large-scale structure of the Universe.
