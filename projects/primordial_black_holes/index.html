Primordial Black Holes As Dark Matter

Black Holes From The Early Universe As A Dark Matter Candidate

Primordial black holes are one of the oldest and most fascinating dark matter candidates: black holes formed not from collapsing stars, but from the collapse of large density fluctuations in the very early Universe. If they exist, they connect the physics of inflation to present-day astrophysical observations in a uniquely direct way.

A recurring difficulty in this field is that observational constraints are usually derived assuming all primordial black holes have the same mass, while realistic formation scenarios predict extended mass distributions. I developed a widely used method to consistently translate constraints from monochromatic to extended mass functions (Bellomo et al., 2018), which has become a standard tool for assessing whether a given formation scenario is viable.

I am also interested in the inverse problem: given an observed abundance of primordial black holes, what does it tell us about the primordial curvature power spectrum — and therefore about inflation itself (Kalaja et al., 2019)?
