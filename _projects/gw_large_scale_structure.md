Where do the binary black holes detected by LIGO and Virgo come from? Are they the remnants of massive stars, or could some of them be primordial? A powerful way to answer this question is statistical: different formation channels predict that mergers live in different cosmic environments, and therefore cluster differently with respect to galaxies.

By cross-correlating gravitational-wave catalogs with galaxy surveys we can measure this clustering and chase the progenitors of merging binary black holes {% cite Scelfo:2018sny %}. Stellar-origin binaries should trace star-forming galaxies, while primordial black holes would preferentially populate low-mass halos — leaving distinct signatures in the cross-correlation signal.

This program will come into its own with the next generation of surveys and detectors, when hundreds of thousands of mergers can be combined with deep galaxy catalogs from facilities such as the SKA Observatory, Euclid, and the Rubin Observatory.
